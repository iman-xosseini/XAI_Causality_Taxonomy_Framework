### a. Explanation of the Failure

The Heat Dissipation Failure is attributed to inadequate cooling, primarily due to a temperature difference of 8.1 K (below the 8.6 K threshold) and a rotational speed of 1275 RPM (below 1380 RPM). In this context, the causal relationships indicate that high tool wear and reduced rotational speed are the most influential factors, with effects on temperature inefficiency (effect strengths: Tool Wear on Rotational Speed = 0.65, Torque on Rotational Speed = -0.32). Insufficient cooling exacerbates the issue, possibly leading to the temperature inefficiency that ultimately results in failure.

### b. Actionable Recommendation

To address Heat Dissipation Failures, start by analyzing thermal sensor data to pinpoint signs of inefficient cooling and potential overheating early on. Next, develop models that align machining parameters, such as rotational speed and tool wear, with heat profiles for optimal cooling strategies. Ensure that these models are capable of predicting and preventing failures in real time by incorporating causal relationships and effect strengths into the analysis. This approach will help in suggesting machining adjustments to maintain effective heat dissipation.
