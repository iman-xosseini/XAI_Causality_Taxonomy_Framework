### a. Explanation of the Failure

Your machine experienced a **Heat Dissipation Failure** because the cooling wasn't effective enough. The key issues were the temperature difference (8.3 K) between the air and the process and a lower speed of 1330 RPM, when both should be higher to avoid overheating. The combination of rotational speed and tool wear plays a big role. Low speed affects wear negatively, leading to inefficient cooling. Think of it as the machine getting hotter than it should because the air hasn’t managed to cool it down properly and the parts were working slower than needed.

### b. Actionable Recommendation

To prevent this from happening again, make sure the cooling system is always filled with coolant and working well. Regularly check that the coolant nozzles are pointed right so the liquid can reach where it's needed. Watch out for any heat warnings from the sensors; if you get one, pause the machine to cool it down if it gets too hot. This can help you maintain the machine better and avoid stops due to overheating.
