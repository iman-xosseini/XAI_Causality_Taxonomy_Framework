### a. Explanation of the Failure

The Heat Dissipation Failure occurred because the machine's parts weren't cooling properly. The temperature difference was too small (7.7 K) and the rotational speed was too low (1358 RPM), which are below the safe limits. This means the machine couldn't get rid of heat effectively, leading to overheating. The wear on the tool (Tool Wear = 61) increased stress, further affecting speed and temperature. The key factor here was the low rotational speed's strong connection with wear, causing the system to fail in managing the heat.

### b. Actionable Recommendation

To prevent this issue, make sure the machine's MQL (Minimum Quantity Lubrication) systems always have enough coolant and are working properly. Regularly check that the coolant nozzles are pointing in the right direction for good cooling. Pay attention to temperature alerts from sensors. If the machine heats up too much, stop and let it cool down before continuing. Consistently doing these actions will help keep the machine running smoothly and safely.
