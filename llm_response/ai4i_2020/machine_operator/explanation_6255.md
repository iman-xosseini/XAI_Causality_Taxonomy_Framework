### a. Explanation of the Failure

The failure called "Overstrain Failure" occurred because the machine's tool wear and torque product (Wear to Torque) exceeded 11,000. The tool wear was high at 204 units, and the rotational speed was elevated at 1379 RPM, causing the machine to overstrain. High tool wear increases strain, and faster speeds contribute to wear, leading to failure. This was amplified by the Wear to Torque value of 11,689, which indicates excessive wear caused by the torque being applied, surpassing safe operational levels for the Low product type.

### b. Actionable Recommendation

To prevent future issues, always operate the machine within the recommended torque and speed limits. Be extra careful when the machine is under heavy load to avoid pushing it too hard. If you notice anything unusual, like vibrations or alarms going off, do report these to the maintenance team right away. Keeping the machine within these guidelines will help avoid overstraining and keep it running smoothly.
