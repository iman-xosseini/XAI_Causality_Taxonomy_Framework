### a. Explanation of the Failure

The Heat Dissipation Failure happened because the machine's cooling wasn't enough. The temperature difference between the air and the process got too low, around 8.4 K, and the machine's rotational speed dropped below 1380, to about 1356. High tool wear also contributed to the problem by affecting the machine's heat balance and speed. Essentially, not enough heat was being carried away by the air, probably worsened by worn-out tools, leading to overheating.

---

### b. Actionable Recommendation

To prevent future overheating issues, make sure the cooling system, especially the MQL (Minimum Quantity Lubricant) system, is always filled with coolant and working properly. Check the nozzles regularly to ensure they are positioned correctly for effective cooling. Keep an eye on temperature alerts from sensors. If temperatures rise above safe levels, pause the machine to let it cool down. This will help avoid similar problems in the future.
